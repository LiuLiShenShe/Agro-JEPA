## 0. Headline

- Global Δt permutation (never done in S3) increases validation MSE by **+11.46%** on average over 20 seeds (range +11.23% to +11.61%), versus **+11.44%** for a constant train-mean interval.
- The S3 batch-local number (**+0.41%**) is retained as history but is not valid evidence about interval sensitivity.
- Plot-cluster bootstrap: the reduction vs persistence is +48.23% [95% CI +47.97%, +48.49%].

### 1.2 Global permutation over the whole validation set

Method: one permutation of the real delta_days over the whole validation set; z_a, z_b and labels unchanged. n = 15,175 pairs / 380 plots; seeds `10000…10019`; RNG `numpy.random.default_rng(seed).permutation(n)`. the global permutation is sliced by the sequential DataLoader offset, so the permutation is not batch-local.

Per-seed values are in `reports/s3_1_diagnostic_audit.json` (`delta_permutation.global_permutation.per_seed`).

**Interpretation (bounded).** A global permutation destroys the pairing between the current image and its interval. The mean increase quantifies how much the model uses the time channel at all; it is a descriptive difference, not a significance test. Multiple seeds give a spread, not a p-value. The constant-interval condition remains the stronger stress test.

## 3. What changes and what does not

1. **Unchanged:** V0 beats persistence and the train-mean baseline; the predicted features remain more useful than the current feature for CC and PH. None of the S3 numbers or checkpoints were altered.
2. **Changed:** the S3 claim that the model is nearly insensitive to Δt (0.41%) was an artefact of batch-local permutation. Under a global permutation the MSE increase is +11.46%, and a constant interval costs +11.44%. The two are statistically indistinguishable here: the time channel contributes a broad growth-level adjustment, but the model does not appear to resolve the precise per-sample interval. Interval precision remains a limitation, not a strength.
3. **Uncertainty:** plot-level bootstrap CIs are reported above; the probe bootstrap uses regenerated per-sample predictions, never back-computed from aggregates.
